Each on-screen string or picture, and the SOURCES item in `script/moai-reshaped.txt` it rests on. "LH25" is Lipo & Hunt 2025, J. Archaeol. Sci. 183, 106383. A7: `factcheck/moai-reshaped.json` (15/15 confirmed) covers the spoken script only; this file covers the pictures and the overlays, which had their own A7 pass (below).

Built 2026-11-24 as one 3D world (`tools/blender/moai_world.py` → `public/3d/moai-world.mp4`, with `public/3d/moai-world-b.mp4` for half-second cross-dissolves at each cut, as in cleopatra-pyramid). Labels, width bars, lean lines and counters are Remotion overlays pinned to the statues through `src/generated/moai-world.anchors.json`. Shapes are procedural; surfaces are CC0 Poly Haven scans, credited in `public/SOURCES.json`.

**The statues** are generic moai lofted from a profile (long head, heavy brow, long nose, pouting lips, long ears, hands on the belly), not a portrait of any statue (C6). No statue is shown broken or damaged. Proportions follow LH25 only qualitatively: road statues have a base about as wide as the shoulders (drawn 0.99 of the shoulder width) and lean forward (drawn 9°, inside LH25's 6–15°); platform statues stand upright with a narrower base (drawn 0.88 of the shoulder width; 0.84 at first, which A7 pass 1 found roughly doubled the gap of even the unverified corrected means) and the front of the base cut back. **These are not the corrected Zenodo means** (SOURCES 7), which A7 could not open; no ratio or angle is written on screen, and every shot with a width bar says "proportions not to a measured ratio". The road statue 12-220-01 (7.35 m tall, 2.83 m wide; LH25 1.4) sets the general shape.

**Gold ghosts** are never stone: a gold outline, see-through, used three times, and each time the small print says what it is. (1) The hook: the road shape over a platform statue, "As it arrived? · their reading". (2) The trimming: the road shape left standing while the statue inside it loses its lean and the front of its base. (3) The close: upright statues where fallen ones lie, "Another reading".

**The ancient walk** now shows islanders on the ropes (owner's reference videos), 7 on each side rope and 8 on the rear rope: LH25 1.5 models the crew: "medium moai between 4 and 6 m required 20–35 people" to start, "an additional 5 to 10 personnel ... to hold the moai from the back", and "once oscillation was established, much smaller teams could maintain movement"; 22 people on a 6 m statue, 8 on the rear rope, sits inside that model (A7 pass 3 corrected this note). The ropes are drawn shorter than LH25's modelled 20–30 m so the crews stay in frame; the small print says "crew size illustrative". They are generic figures, bare-chested with a plain undyed bark-cloth wrap, no faces, no tattoos or ornaments: nothing is claimed about dress (C6). A7 pass 3: acceptable; bark cloth (mahute) is attested on Rapa Nui at Cook's 1774 visit (Pitt Rivers Museum, Forster collection, 1886.1.1250). **The test** shows people because LH25 1.4 says how many and where: "four on each lateral rope and ten on the rear rope". They are plain mannequins in modern work clothes, with no faces and a range of skin tones; nobody is a portrait (C4). The test site is a plain field: the place is not shown.

**A7 pass 1 (2026-11-24, visuals; an agent that did not make the short, working from the stills, the overlay code and the sources).** Five fixes, four caveats; all applied as below. The sources it opened are cited here because they are not in the script's SOURCES block (editing the script would void its fact-check hash); they are that pass's readings, not re-read here.
- **Fallen statues were the wrong way round. Fixed.** LH25 2.5: "the outward-facing orientations of fallen road moai"; LH25 1.3: "the majority are found face-down when roads slope downhill and frequently on their backs when going uphill". A statue facing outward that falls forward lies face down with its head outward; one that falls back lies face up with its head toward the quarry. The first build had both reversed. Which way each one fell is not tied to the slope of the road in the model.
- **Roads: now about 4.5 m wide and concave in section** (LH25 abstract and 1.3: "averaging approximately 4.5 m in width ... the roadbeds have concave cross-sections (Love, 2001)"). They had been drawn 6 m wide and flat, and listed here as unsourced.
- **Carving away: the gold outline must be gone before the statue straightens.** The first build straightened it inside the outline, so the head ended up outside the "before" shape: stone that carving cannot add. Now: the base is cut back inside the gold road shape, the gold fades, then the statue is set upright. LH25 1.1: "Carvers removed material from the front of the base, shifting the center of mass back over the base center to achieve stable upright positioning." LH25 does not say whether this happened on the platform or beside it; it is drawn on the platform ("illustration").
- **"No eye sockets": the brow's shadow read as sockets.** That shot now has a high sun, so the area under the brow is lit.
- **Platform base 0.84 → 0.88** (above).
- **The platform (ahu): sourced.** PBS NOVA transcript, *Secrets of Easter Island*: "Most ahu platforms are along the coast." / "The moai face inland, their backs to the sea." (pbs.org/wgbh/nova/transcripts/27eieaster.html). A documentary transcript quoting archaeologists, not a paper: UNESCO and Britannica refused automated reads. The stonework is generic.
- **The replica's size: plausible.** National Geographic (2012): "ten-foot (three-meter), five-ton moai replica" (nationalgeographic.com/culture/article/120622-easter-island-statues-moved-hunt-lipo-science-rocked). Drawn 2.96 m, scaled from the statue shape at an assumed 2.3 t/m³; LH25 1.4 says it "retained the same proportions and mass distribution as the original". The screen shows LH25's 4.35 t, not National Geographic's "five-ton". The small print still says "replica's size estimated from its weight". No size is written on screen.
- **Rano Raraku: caveat, now fixed.** The island has been rebuilt with its triangular outline (Rano Kau south-west, Terevaka north, Poike east) and Rano Raraku just inland of the south-east coast; the coast is freehand and the map says "map simplified, not to scale". Drawn with its crater lake (same source). A crater of volcanic tuff that was the main quarry (USGS / PLoS ONE 2021, doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0254793: "Rano Raraku, the crater lake constrained by basaltic tuff that served as the primary quarry"), but it is "on the eastern side" of the island (UCLA newsroom), not mid-island as drawn. The small print now says "island simplified, not to scale".
- **Eye sockets: now independently supported.** Claudio Cristino in the same NOVA transcript: moai were finished at the quarry "except for the eye sockets which were opened when the moai was erected on top of the ahu". LH25 gives no citation for its sentence (SOURCES 8).
- **"Set up on purpose" ghosts: caveat.** They face outward, as the critics say road moai do (LH25 2.5: "the consistent outward-facing orientation of road moai"). They stand on bare ground, though LH25 reports the critics also cite "possible platform structures beneath some statues", and they keep the road statues' forward lean. "A reading, not a reconstruction" carries both.

**A7 pass 3 (2026-11-24, visuals after the rework; a separate agent, from 22 stills, the code and the sources).** Three fixes, four caveats, applied:
- **Map icons were evenly spaced along the roads; LH25 has them packed near the quarry. Fixed.** LH25 Fig. 12: "65.5 % within 2 km and 42.9 % within just 1 km" (median 1.39 km); 2.5: "79.8 % ... within 3.5 km of the quarry ... strong clustering near the quarry rather than regular spacing". The 62 icons (LH25's count) are now placed by that distribution. Even spacing would have drawn the critics' pattern instead of the data. The 3D statues seen up close are not moved.
- **"Old roads" pin** sat on grass beside the road; now on the drawn road.
- **"4 · side rope"** was cut off at the frame edge; moved in.
- **Roads: caveat.** Lipo & Hunt 2005 mapped "32 km of seven major roads" (*Antiquity* 79); the map draws three, the south-coast one confirmed by LH25 Fig. 10. Small print now "map simplified, not to scale · main roads only".
- **Closing: caveat.** The ghosts now fade to 40 % on "What do you think?", so both readings stay on screen.
- **Gold small print** now shows only while a gold shape is on screen.
- **Face-up road statue:** camera raised so brow and nose read.
- Island layout confirmed approximately (NASA Earth Observatory on Poike; positions relative to Rano Raraku within about 2 km), inside "not to scale".

**A7 pass 4 (2026-11-25, the full-island map; a separate agent, from raw and finished stills, against OpenStreetMap's coastline projected about Rano Raraku, real area 163.5 km²).** The first map outline was a flat sliver (south-west corner 2.7 km too far north), Rano Kau and Terevaka were 1–3 km off, the map showed for about 3 s before any "not to scale" wording, and the "Old roads" pin sat off the road. Fixed: the map beat now shows its own island, traced closer to the real outline, with Terevaka, Rano Kau (and its crater lake), Poike and Rano Raraku at their projected positions, Motu Nui and Motu Iti off Rano Kau; the south-coast road kept along the coast; the north road reaching the coast; the icons' tail cut to LH25's 8.7 % beyond 4.5 km; the camera holds on the whole island until "roads". The ground-level world keeps its simpler outline: it is never seen whole. Caveat: Rano Aroi, a third crater lake on Terevaka's flank, is not drawn; nothing on screen says there are only two. **Outline now traced from NASA imagery (owner, 2026-11-25).** To keep OpenStreetMap (ODbL, not on the D1 list) out of the render, the map island is traced from public-domain NASA GIBS imagery by `tools/blender/trace_rapa_nui.py` into `tools/blender/rapa_nui_trace.json`: the coastline from the SRTM land mask (108 points, 120 m tolerance; area 166.7 km², the published figure is 163.6 km², the pixel-edge trace runs slightly outside), Terevaka and Poike at the SRTM highs, Rano Kau's crater lake from the Landsat WELD 2000 composite (drawn at 480 m radius), and three islets (Motu Nui and Motu Iti merge at this resolution, plus two rocks off the west coast). The Landsat image puts Rano Raraku's crater on LH25's quarry coordinate (-27.125175, -109.288170), which anchors the projection. The south-coast road now runs ~650 m inside the traced coast; the scene refuses to build if any map road comes within 150 m of the sea, and `pin()` refuses a name that is already an object (A7 pass 5 found two pins tracking the wrong objects that way).

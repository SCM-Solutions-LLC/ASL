The Altar Stone at the heart of Stonehenge came from northeast Scotland.
It is a six-tonne slab of sandstone, nearly five metres long.
Several of Stonehenge's bluestones came from hills in west Wales, about two hundred and twenty-five kilometres away.
For about a hundred years, the Altar Stone was thought to come from Wales too.
Then geologists dated tiny mineral grains inside it.
Their ages matched old sandstone in northeast Scotland, at least seven hundred and fifty kilometres away.
How six tonnes made that trip is unknown. The researchers think it probably went by sea.

SOURCES (for approval — every claim in the script, with the number behind it)

Primary paper, read at the publisher on 2026-11-17 (nature.com, fetched through
the Firecrawl scraper because this sandbox cannot reach nature.com directly):
Clarke, Kirkland, Bevins, Pearce, Glorie & Ixer, "A Scottish provenance for the
Altar Stone of Stonehenge", Nature 632, 570-575 (2024; online 14 August 2024),
doi:10.1038/s41586-024-07652-1. Called "Clarke" below.

1. "The Altar Stone at the heart of Stonehenge came from northeast Scotland."
   Clarke, abstract: "Detrital age comparisons to sedimentary packages
   throughout Britain and Ireland reveal a remarkable similarity to the Old Red
   Sandstone of the Orcadian Basin in northeast Scotland." Title: "A Scottish
   provenance for the Altar Stone". Body: "the central megalith of Stonehenge,
   the Altar Stone (Stone 80)". The script says "northeast Scotland" and no
   closer: the paper names a basin, not a quarry or outcrop.

2. "It is a six-tonne slab of sandstone, nearly five metres long."
   Clarke, abstract: "the Altar Stone, a 6 tonne shaped block"; "the central
   recumbent sandstone megalith". Body: "measuring 4.9 x 1.0 x 0.5 m".

3. "Several of Stonehenge's bluestones came from hills in west Wales, about two
   hundred and twenty-five kilometres away."
   Clarke: "the igneous bluestones were brought around 225 km from the Mynydd
   Preseli to Stonehenge" (citing Parker Pearson et al. 2020). Clarke also
   calls the Altar Stone "the largest of the bluestones". Mynydd Preseli is in
   Pembrokeshire, west Wales; Clarke places the Mill Bay site "close to the
   Mynydd Preseli source of the doleritic and rhyolitic bluestones". "Several"
   is deliberately vague: the script does not count the Welsh stones.

4. "For about a hundred years, the Altar Stone was thought to come from Wales
   too."
   Clarke: "An early proposed source for the Altar Stone from Mill Bay,
   Pembrokeshire ... strongly influenced the notion of a sea transport route
   via the Bristol Channel", citing H. H. Thomas, "The source of the stones of
   Stonehenge", Antiquaries Journal 3, 239-260 (1923),
   doi:10.1017/S0003581500005096. After Mill Bay was ruled out, "a source from
   elsewhere in the ORS of the Anglo-Welsh Basin was still considered likely"
   (Bevins et al., J. Archaeol. Sci. 120, 105188 (2020)), until Bevins et al.
   2023 (J. Archaeol. Sci. Rep. 51, 104215) showed "the Anglo-Welsh Basin is
   highly unlikely to be the source". 1923 to 2023 is a hundred years. Thomas
   and the two Bevins papers were not opened; this entry rests on Clarke's
   account of them.

5. "Then geologists dated tiny mineral grains inside it."
   Clarke: "we present in situ U-Pb, Lu-Hf and trace element isotopic data for
   zircon, apatite and rutile from two fragments of the Altar Stone"; the grains
   are "typically 10-40 µm wide".

6. "Their ages matched old sandstone in northeast Scotland, at least seven
   hundred and fifty kilometres away."
   Clarke, abstract (claim 1), and: "Such a provenance implies that the Altar
   Stone ... was sourced at least 750 km from its current location." "Old
   sandstone" stands for the Old Red Sandstone, which is Devonian. Not said
   aloud: "old" is a plain word here, not a date.

7. "How six tonnes made that trip is unknown. The researchers think it probably
   went by sea."
   Clarke, abstract: "The difficulty of long-distance overland transport of such
   massive cargo from Scotland, navigating topographic barriers, suggests that
   it was transported by sea." Publisher summary: "probably by sea". The paper
   gives no direct evidence of the route, so "unknown" stands.

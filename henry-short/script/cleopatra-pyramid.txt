Cleopatra lived closer to the Moon landing than to the building of the Great Pyramid.
So what did the pyramid look like in her day? Not this.
It still wore its smooth, polished outer casing.
A Greek historian, Diodorus, visited Egypt when Cleopatra was a girl. He wrote that its stones were still in place.
He was told three hundred and sixty thousand men spent twenty years building it. He wrote that an inscription listed what the workers' vegetables and laxatives cost: over sixteen hundred talents.
Then, in 2013, at an ancient harbour on the Red Sea, archaeologists found papyri from the reign of the pyramid's own king, Khufu. The oldest papyri with writing ever found in Egypt.
Among them was the logbook of Merer's boat crew, hauling limestone from a quarry seventeen kilometres away to the pyramid.
They lay buried for about four and a half thousand years.
To Cleopatra, the pyramid was ancient history. We can read the work log of a crew that helped build it.
So what are we leaving behind that someone will read four thousand years from now?

1. "Cleopatra lived closer to the Moon landing than to the building of the
   Great Pyramid."
   Unchanged from cleopatra-timeline line 1; see its SOURCES 1-5: about 2,531
   years back to c. 2600 BC against 2,037 forward to AD 1969 from her birth in
   69 BC. Holds on every published chronology for Khufu.

2. "So what did the pyramid look like in her day? Not this."
   "Not this" is said over a picture of the pyramid as it is today, with its
   stepped core showing. The difference is lines 3-4: in antiquity the outside
   was polished, fitted stone. Says "in her day", never that she saw it: there
   is no record that Cleopatra visited Giza (A5).

3. "It still wore its smooth, polished outer casing."
   Herodotus 2.124.5 (5th c. BC): "the whole is of stone polished and most
   exactly fitted". Diodorus 1.63.5 (line 4): "the stones remain to this day
   still preserving their original position and the entire structure
   undecayed". "Casing": Hawass (NOVA) "the casing stones that came from Tura";
   Lehner (NOVA) "the casing stones which were custom cut and set, one to
   another". Not said: "white" (only in Lehner's NOVA interview) and the
   original height (146.6 m is from Lehner 1997 via Wikipedia, unread; Petrie
   1883 unreachable from this session). Add either only once read.

4. "A Greek historian, Diodorus, visited Egypt when Cleopatra was a girl. He
   wrote that its stones were still in place."
   Diodorus 1.44.1: kings ruled "down to the One Hundred and Eightieth
   Olympiad, the time when we visited Egypt and the king was Ptolemy, who took
   the name of The New Dionysus". The 180th Olympiad is 60/59-57/56 BC; born in
   69 BC (Rosso 2021), Cleopatra was about 9 to 13. Ptolemy XII was her father
   (Rosso 2021: "born at 69 BC from an illegitimate union of Ptolemy XII").
   "Still in place": 1.63.5, quoted in 3. "Its" is the largest pyramid,
   "constructed" by "Chemmis" (1.63.2), i.e. the Great Pyramid.

5. "He was told three hundred and sixty thousand men spent twenty years
   building it. He wrote that an inscription listed what the workers'
   vegetables and laxatives cost: over sixteen hundred talents."
   Diodorus 1.63.9: "three hundred and sixty thousand men, as they say, were
   employed on the undertaking, and the whole structure was scarcely completed
   in twenty years" ("as they say" = "he was told"). 1.64.3: "an inscription on
   the larger pyramid gives the sum of money expended on it, since the writing
   sets forth that on vegetables and purgatives for the workmen there were paid
   out over sixteen hundred talents" ("purgatives" = laxatives). "He wrote":
   this is Diodorus's report of the inscription, not a surviving find. No "of
   silver": that is Herodotus 2.125.6, whose telling differs (fact-check
   2026-11-22). The modern workforce comparison was cut: no A2 source read.

6. "Then, in 2013, at an ancient harbour on the Red Sea, archaeologists found
   papyri from the reign of the pyramid's own king, Khufu. The oldest papyri
   with writing ever found in Egypt."
   Tallet & Marouard 2014 (abstract): "In 2013 the discovery of several dozen
   fragments of papyrus, the oldest inscribed papyri ever unearthed in Egypt,
   confirmed the early date of this site and revealed a final closure of this
   installation at the end of the reign of Khufu"; title: "The Harbor of Khufu
   on the Red Sea Coast at Wadi al-Jarf". Tallet & Marouard 2016: papyri that
   "name King Khufu", terminus "year 26-27". "The pyramid's own king": Sheisha
   2022, "the Great Pyramid of Giza (Khufu Pyramid)". "Ever found in Egypt"
   keeps the source's scope, as of 2014.

7. "Among them was the logbook of Merer's boat crew, hauling limestone from a
   quarry seventeen kilometres away to the pyramid."
   Tallet & Marouard 2016 n. 2: "logs from a team of sailors shipping blocks of
   limestone from Tura to Giza", published as "Le « Journal de Merer »".
   Sheisha 2022: "The Journal of Merer, a large corpus of these papyri,
   describes the transport of limestone from Toura, ∼17 km from the Giza
   Plateau, to the construction site of the Great Pyramid of Khufu."

8. "They lay buried for about four and a half thousand years."
   "Unearthed" in 2013 (Tallet & Marouard 2014); the harbour closed at the end
   of Khufu's reign (same). End of his reign: median 2565-2603 BC in Erdil
   2025's models, so 4,577-4,615 years to AD 2013; about 4,470 on Spence's low
   dates (cleopatra-timeline SOURCES 1). "About four and a half thousand"
   covers both.

9. "To Cleopatra, the pyramid was ancient history. We can read the work log
    of a crew that helped build it."
    First sentence: cleopatra-timeline SOURCES 6. Second: 6-7; Merer's crew
    shipped limestone to "the construction site of the Great Pyramid of Khufu"
    (Sheisha 2022). No claim about what Cleopatra knew or could read.

10. "So what are we leaving behind that someone will read four thousand years
    from now?"
    A question to the viewer, not a claim. Not engagement bait (E2): it asks
    for no action and follows from 8-9.

Cleopatra lived closer to the Moon landing than to the building of the Great Pyramid.
So what did the pyramid look like in her day? Not this.
It still wore its smooth, polished outer casing.
A Greek historian, Diodorus, visited Egypt when Cleopatra was a girl. He wrote that its stones were still in place.
He was told three hundred and sixty thousand men spent twenty years building it, and that an inscription listed what the workers' vegetables and laxatives cost: over sixteen hundred talents.
Archaeologists today put the workforce at a tenth of that, or less.
But at an ancient port on the Red Sea, archaeologists have found papyri from the time of the pyramid's own king, Khufu.
Among them is the Journal of Merer, describing limestone moved from a quarry about seventeen kilometres away to the pyramid.
Those papyri are about four and a half thousand years old.
To Cleopatra, the pyramid was ancient history. We can read a journal from the time it was being built.
So what are we leaving behind that someone will read four thousand years from now?
---

1. "Cleopatra lived closer to the Moon landing than to the building of the
   Great Pyramid."
   Unchanged from cleopatra-timeline line 1; see its SOURCES 1-5: about 2,531
   years back to c. 2600 BC against 2,037 forward to AD 1969 from her birth in
   69 BC. Holds on every published chronology for Khufu.

2. "So what did the pyramid look like in her day? Not this."
   "Not this" is said over a picture of the pyramid as it is today, with its
   stepped core showing. The difference is lines 3-4: in antiquity the outside
   was polished, fitted stone. Says "in her day", never that she saw it: there
   is no record that Cleopatra visited Giza (A5).

3. "It still wore its smooth, polished outer casing."
   Herodotus 2.124.5 (5th c. BC): "the whole is of stone polished and most
   exactly fitted". Diodorus 1.63.5 (line 4): "the stones remain to this day
   still preserving their original position and the entire structure
   undecayed". "Casing": Hawass (NOVA) "the casing stones that came from Tura";
   Lehner (NOVA) "the casing stones which were custom cut and set, one to
   another". Not said: "white" (only in Lehner's NOVA interview) and the
   original height (146.6 m is from Lehner 1997 via Wikipedia, unread; Petrie
   1883 unreachable from this session). Add either only once read.

4. "A Greek historian, Diodorus, visited Egypt when Cleopatra was a girl. He
   wrote that its stones were still in place."
   Diodorus 1.44.1: kings ruled "down to the One Hundred and Eightieth
   Olympiad, the time when we visited Egypt and the king was Ptolemy, who took
   the name of The New Dionysus". The 180th Olympiad is 60/59-57/56 BC; born in
   69 BC (Rosso 2021), Cleopatra was about 9 to 13. Ptolemy XII was her father
   (Rosso 2021: "born at 69 BC from an illegitimate union of Ptolemy XII").
   "Still in place": 1.63.5, quoted in 3. "Its" is the largest pyramid,
   "constructed" by "Chemmis" (1.63.2), i.e. the Great Pyramid.

5. "He was told three hundred and sixty thousand men spent twenty years
   building it, and that an inscription listed what the workers' vegetables
   and laxatives cost: over sixteen hundred talents."
   Diodorus 1.63.9: "three hundred and sixty thousand men, as they say, were
   employed on the undertaking, and the whole structure was scarcely completed
   in twenty years" ("as they say" = "he was told"). 1.64.3: "an inscription on
   the larger pyramid gives the sum of money expended on it, since the writing
   sets forth that on vegetables and purgatives for the workmen there were paid
   out over sixteen hundred talents" ("purgatives" = laxatives). "Of silver"
   was cut: it is Herodotus 2.125.6, a different telling (exactly 1,600;
   purges, onions and garlic), and the line is credited to Diodorus.

6. "Archaeologists today put the workforce at a tenth of that, or less."
   A tenth of 360,000 is 36,000. Lehner 2015 reviews the published estimates
   for Khufu's workforce: from a low of about 2,000 up to 36,000 (Borchardt &
   Croon, accepted by Verner). The top of that range is exactly a tenth; the
   rest is less. NOVA interviews (Hawass 36,000; Lehner about 5,000 at the
   core, 20,000-30,000 in all) agree, as pointers only.

7. "But at an ancient port on the Red Sea, archaeologists have found papyri
   from the time of the pyramid's own king, Khufu."
   Sheisha 2022: "the Wadi el-Jarf Papyri, discovered at a Khufu-age port on
   the Red Sea coast". "The pyramid's own king": Sheisha 2022, "the Great
   Pyramid of Khufu". No year and no "oldest" claim: those rest on Tallet &
   Marouard 2014, not read.

8. "Among them is the Journal of Merer, describing limestone moved from a
   quarry about seventeen kilometres away to the pyramid."
   Sheisha 2022: "The Journal of Merer, a large corpus of these papyri,
   describes the transport of limestone from Toura, ~17 km from the Giza
   Plateau, to the construction site of the Great Pyramid of Khufu." Says
   nothing about who Merer was, a boat or a crew, so neither does the line.

9. "Those papyri are about four and a half thousand years old."
   Khufu-age (7). Sheisha 2022: Khufu's accession 2613-2577 BCE (14C) down
   to 2480 +/- 5 BCE (Spence); Khafre's no later than 2448 +/- 5 BCE. So about
   4,470-4,640 years before AD 2026; "about four and a half thousand" covers
   the whole range. Age only; no claim about how they were preserved.

10. "To Cleopatra, the pyramid was ancient history. We can read a journal from
    the time it was being built."
    First sentence: cleopatra-timeline SOURCES 6. Second: 8; the journal
    describes limestone going to "the construction site of the Great Pyramid
    of Khufu" (Sheisha 2022), so it dates from the build. No claim about what
    Cleopatra knew or could read.

11. "So what are we leaving behind that someone will read four thousand years
    from now?"
    A question to the viewer, not a claim. Not engagement bait (E2): it asks
    for no action and follows from 9-10.

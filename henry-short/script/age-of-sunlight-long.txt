Right now, two things from the Sun's core are reaching the Earth.
One left the core about eight minutes ago.
The other left it tens of thousands of years ago, maybe far more.
Both start where hydrogen fuses into helium.
Every second, the Sun turns over four million tonnes of its mass into energy.
And every time four hydrogen nuclei become one helium nucleus, two neutrinos are made.
Neutrinos barely interact with anything.
They fly straight out of the Sun in about two seconds.
Eight minutes later, they reach the Earth, and pass through you.
Tens of billions of them cross every square centimetre of you, every second.
In Italy, a detector called Borexino counted the ones from fusion's very first step.
In every hundred tonnes of its liquid, it caught about a hundred and forty a day.
The energy takes the slow road.
It is absorbed and given off again, over and over, then carried up by churning gas.
Tens of thousands of years later, maybe far more, it leaves the surface as sunlight.
Eight minutes after that, it lands on your skin.
So the sunlight shows what the core was doing when that energy set out.
The neutrinos show what it was doing eight minutes ago.
In twenty fourteen, Borexino's count matched what the sunlight predicts, to within about ten percent.
So the core is burning about as hard now as when today's sunlight set out.
The sunlight on your skin is old news. The neutrinos are fresh. And they agree.

1. "Right now, two things from the Sun's core are reaching the Earth."
   The two are the neutrinos made by fusion in the core (claims 6-11) and the energy that fusion
   releases, which reaches the Earth as sunlight (MSFC, above: the core's reactions "release the
   energy that ultimately leaves the surface as visible light"). Both reach the Earth all the time.
   The hook says "the Earth", not "you": at night the sunlight does not reach you, though the
   neutrinos do.

   NASA MSFC "about a million years" to 0.7 R; Stix, Solar Physics 212, 3 (2003),

8. "They fly straight out of the Sun in about two seconds."
   Straight out: BX-P "almost undisturbed", MSFC "pass right through". Speed: a neutrino's mass is
   under 0.8 eV/c^2 (KATRIN). Even at 10 keV, low for a solar neutrino (the pp spectrum ends at
   420 keV, BX-P), its Lorentz factor is over 10^4 and its speed differs from c by under one part
   in 10^8 (1 - v/c ~ 1/(2 gamma^2)), so it moves at c to any precision that matters here. Time: from the centre, the Sun's
   radius (NSSDC, 695,700 km) / c = 2.32 s; from the edge of the core at 0.25 R (MSFC) about
   1.7 s. "About two seconds."
   On screen: "ABOUT 2 SECONDS"; a clock tag beside the drawing counts from 0.0 S to 2.3 S.

    Follows from claims 1-3: today's sunlight carries energy the core released tens of thousands

19. "In twenty fourteen, Borexino's count matched what the sunlight predicts, to within about ten
    percent."
    BX-N, published online 27 August 2014. BX-P: the measured pp flux, (6.6 ± 0.7) x 10^10 cm^-2 s^-1, is
    "consistent with the SSM prediction". The Standard Solar Model is fitted to the Sun's measured
    (photon) luminosity, which is why BX-P says the agreement "justifies the assumption made by the
    SSM that the photon and neutrino luminosities from the Sun can be correlated". So "what the
    sunlight predicts" is the SSM prediction. The measurement's uncertainty is ±0.7 in 6.6, 11%;
    "to within about ten percent" is the precision of the match, not a claim that they differ by
    that. BX-N's abstract states the same result as power: about 99% of the Sun's 3.84 x 10^33 erg/s
    comes from pp fusion.
    On screen: "WITHIN ABOUT 10%"; two bars, "PER DAY, PER 100 TONNES": "SUNLIGHT PREDICTS" 131
    and "BOREXINO COUNTED" 144 ± 16. BX-P: "we can calculate the expected rate of pp-induced
    electron recoils in Borexino to be 131 ± 2 (d·100 t)^-1 (assuming the high-metallicity SSM)",
    from the SSM's pp flux with the oscillation parameters and the scintillator's properties;
    measured, BX-P eq. (2): 144 ± 13 (stat) ± 10 (syst) per day per 100 t; combined in
    quadrature, ±16 (11%), which is the error bar drawn. 144/131 = 1.10: they differ by about ten
    percent, inside the count's ±16 plus the prediction's ±2.

Only one spacecraft has ever visited Uranus, and it arrived at an unusual time.
Voyager 2 flew past in January 1986.
In the days before, the solar wind had picked up and squeezed the magnetic bubble around the planet.
Researchers estimate it is squeezed that hard only about four percent of the time.
Had Voyager arrived about a week earlier, the solar wind pushing on that bubble would have been about twenty times weaker.
So the extreme magnetic bubble we have described for forty years may just be what one unusual week looked like.

SOURCES (for approval — every claim in the script, with the number behind it)

Primary paper, read at the publisher on 2026-11-17 (nature.com, fetched through
the Firecrawl scraper because this sandbox cannot reach nature.com directly):
Jasinski, Cochrane, Jia, Dunn, Roussos, Nordheim, Regoli, Achilleos, Krupp &
Murphy, "The anomalous state of Uranus's magnetosphere during the Voyager 2
flyby", Nature Astronomy 9, 66-74 (2025; online 11 November 2024),
doi:10.1038/s41550-024-02389-3. Called "Jasinski" below.

1. "Only one spacecraft has ever visited Uranus"
   NASA Science, "Voyager 2" mission page
   (https://science.nasa.gov/mission/voyager/voyager-2/): "Voyager 2 is the only
   spacecraft to visit Uranus and Neptune." Jasinski's abstract calls the
   flyby "This single in situ measurement".

2. "... and it arrived at an unusual time."
   Jasinski, abstract: "Voyager 2 observed Uranus's magnetosphere in an
   anomalous, compressed state that we estimate to be present less than 5% of
   the time." "Unusual" is the plain-English form of "anomalous". It is the
   timing, not the spacecraft, that was unusual, and the hook says no more than
   that.

3. "Voyager 2 flew past in January 1986."
   NASA (claim 1): "Closest approach to Uranus took place at 17:59 UT Jan. 24,
   1986". Jasinski, abstract: "The Voyager 2 flyby of Uranus in 1986".

4. "In the days before, the solar wind had picked up and squeezed the magnetic
   bubble around the planet."
   Jasinski, body: "both the density and dynamic pressure were steadily
   increasing for 2 days ... before the flyby, from ~0.005 nPa to 0.018 nPa".
   Abstract: Voyager 2 saw "an extreme compression of the planet's
   magnetosphere by the upstream solar wind". "Magnetic bubble" is the plain
   word for magnetosphere. The script deliberately says the BUBBLE was
   squeezed, not that the planet's magnetic field was different: the field's
   odd tilt and offset is intrinsic to Uranus and the paper does not claim the
   solar wind caused it.

5. "Researchers estimate it is squeezed that hard only about four percent of
   the time."
   Jasinski, body: "a Voyager 2-observed magnetopause R_SS of 17.3 R_U or lower
   is expected to occur only 4% of the time at Uranus." The abstract rounds it
   to "less than 5% of the time". Both support "about four percent".

6. "Had Voyager arrived about a week earlier, the solar wind pushing on that
   bubble would have been about twenty times weaker."
   Jasinski, body: the low values (0.001 nPa eight days before the flyby, and
   a 0.00078 nPa minimum on day 13, 11 days before) are "~18 to ~23 times
   lower than during the Uranus flyby itself". Conclusions: "Had Voyager 2
   arrived at Uranus a week earlier", the solar wind dynamic pressure would
   have been much weaker. The abstract's looser wording ("only a few days
   earlier ... ~20 times lower") is NOT followed: two days before the flyby
   the pressure was only about four times lower (~0.005 against 0.018 nPa,
   "an almost fourfold increase from day 22"). Per A3 the script takes the
   data-backed pairing: a week, twenty times. "Weaker" stands for "lower
   dynamic pressure"; "that bubble" names what it pushes on, so the line
   cannot be heard as the wind pushing on the spacecraft.

7. "So the extreme magnetic bubble we have described for forty years may just be
   what one unusual week looked like."
   Jasinski, abstract: the flyby "has been the basis of our interpretation of
   Uranus's magnetosphere as the canonical extreme magnetosphere of the solar
   system; with inexplicably intense electron radiation belts and a severely
   plasma-depleted magnetosphere ... the interpretation of Uranus's
   magnetosphere as being extreme may simply be a product of a flyby that
   occurred under extreme upstream solar wind conditions." The script keeps
   the paper's "may". Forty years: 1986 to 2026.
   Scope: the line says "magnetic bubble" (the magnetosphere), which is all the
   paper is about. Uranus's sideways spin and its tilted, off-centre magnetic
   FIELD are real and untouched by this paper, which says its result is
   unlikely to change the conclusions of Connerney et al. about the field.
   Neither the words nor the visuals may suggest those are flukes, or that the
   atmosphere, rings or moons were affected.
   "One unusual week": the paper describes the magnetosphere "continuously
   compressed (from ~28 to ~17 R_U) for the days leading up to the Voyager 2
   flyby", and claim 6 puts the weak wind about a week earlier.

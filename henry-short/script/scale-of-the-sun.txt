Jupiter is the biggest planet in the solar system.
Two and a half times the mass of every other planet combined.
But hold Jupiter up next to the Sun, and it disappears.
You could pour a thousand Jupiters into the Sun and still have room left over.
And here is the part that gets people.
Our Sun is not a big star. It is not even average.
It is heavier and brighter than about ninety percent of the stars in our galaxy,
because most stars are red dwarfs: small, dim, and everywhere.
The Sun only looks ordinary because we have never stood next to anything else.

SOURCES (for approval — every claim in the script, with the number behind it)

1. "Two and a half times the mass of every other planet combined."
   Jupiter 1.898e27 kg; all other planets together ~7.5e26 kg. Ratio ~2.5.
   NASA Jupiter and planetary fact sheets.

2. "A thousand Jupiters into the Sun."
   Sun volume 1.41e18 km^3; Jupiter volume 1.43e15 km^3. Ratio ~986.
   Volume, not mass — the Sun is ~1,048 Jupiter MASSES, which is a different
   (and coincidentally similar) number. The script says "pour into", i.e. volume.

3. "Heavier and brighter than about ninety percent of the stars in our galaxy."
   Weakest number in the script. M-dwarfs are ~73-75% of stars in the Milky Way;
   adding K-dwarfs puts stars smaller than the Sun at roughly 90%. Figures of
   85-95% are all quoted in the literature depending on the sample and the cutoff.
   If a tighter claim is wanted: "smaller than about three quarters of the stars
   in the galaxy are red dwarfs" is safe and uncontested.

4. "Most stars are red dwarfs: small, dim, and everywhere."
   Uncontested.

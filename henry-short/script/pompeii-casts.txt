In Pompeii, a victim found wearing a gold bracelet, beside a small child, was long described as a mother.
Ancient DNA says it was a man, with no sign he was related to the child.
Two victims found close together, read as an embrace, were imagined as sisters, or a mother and daughter.
One of them was genetically male.
The casts were made from 1863 on, by pouring plaster into the hollows the bodies left in the ash.
Over the years, restorers reworked some casts, possibly to make them tell a story.
The DNA does not tell us who these people were. It tells us the old stories were guesses.

Primary paper, full text read on 2026-11-17 through Firecrawl's research index

1. "In Pompeii, a victim found wearing a gold bracelet, beside a small child, was
   long described as a mother."
   Pilli, House of the Golden Bracelet: in 1974 "two adults (Individuals 50 and
   52) and a young child (Individual 51), apparently standing on Individual
   52's hip" were found. "Individual 52 was traditionally interpreted as a
   woman and mother, due to the association with the child, and an intricate
   golden bracelet of exceptional weight (6.1 grams) worn on one arm, which also
   gave the house its name." The script says "beside", not "holding", because
   the paper says only "apparently standing on" the adult's hip. "Long
   described": the reading dates from the 1974 discovery, and Pilli says such
   interpretations were "spread through museal exhibitions and educational and
   publicity materials".

2. "Ancient DNA says it was a man, with no sign he was related to the child."
   Pilli: "All individuals were sexed as genetically male, including the
   presumed female adult (Individual 52)." And: "both the mitochondrial DNA and
   whole genome data found no evidence of biological relatedness, at least up to
   the third degree, between any of the individuals". The script's "no sign he
   was related" is that finding, worded so it does not claim more than "up to
   the third degree".

3. "Two victims found close together, read as an embrace, were imagined as
   sisters, or a mother and daughter."
   The embrace is itself an interpretation (the paper's "interpreted as an
   embrace"), so the script reports it as a reading, not as how they were
   found.
   Pilli, House of the Cryptoporticus (excavated 1914): "two (Individuals 21 and
   22) were found close to each other in what was interpreted as an embrace ...
   the archaeologists hypothesized that they could be two sisters, mother and
   daughter, or lovers". The script leaves out "lovers" for length; it is not
   needed for the correction.

4. "One of them was genetically male."
   Pilli: "The nuclear genetic analysis was successful only for Individual 22
   and revealed he was male, excluding the possibility that the pair of victims
   were sisters or mother and daughter." The script says "one of them" because
   only one of the two could be sexed.

5. "The casts were made from 1863 on, by pouring plaster into the hollows the
   bodies left in the ash."
   Pilli, Methods: "In 1863 Fiorelli set up a method to realize plaster casts on
   some of the victims of the eruption, pouring liquid chalk into the voids left
   by the bodies in the hardened ash." Introduction: "pouring liquid plaster
   into the voids left by decay of the victims' soft tissue". The two casts in
   this script were made after the 1914 and 1974 finds, hence "from 1863 on".

6. "Over the years, restorers reworked some casts, possibly to make them tell a
   story."
   The paper speaks of the casts in general; it does not say these two casts
   were reworked, or reworked to tell the mother or sisters stories. So the
   line says "some casts" and "a story", not "them" and "those stories".
   Pilli, Introduction: "The casts had been considerably manipulated and likely
   creatively restored in the past". Discussion: "it is possible that the
   exploitation of the casts as vehicles for storytelling led to the
   manipulation of their poses and relative positioning by restorers in the
   past." The script keeps the paper's "possible" as "possibly".

7. "The DNA does not tell us who these people were. It tells us the old stories
   were guesses."
   Pilli: the study tested "interpretations suggested in the absence of genetic
   data about the identity of the victims and their relations to each other,
   based on the shape and position of the bodies", and "illustrates how
   unreliable narratives based on limited evidence can be". The authors also
   warn against "establishing new narratives that might also misrepresent these
   people's lived experiences", which is why the script ends without offering a
   new story.

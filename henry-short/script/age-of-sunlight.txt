The energy in the sunlight on your skin spent tens of thousands of years, maybe far more, getting out of the Sun.
The trip from the Sun to you took eight minutes.
Getting out was the slow part.
But it is not the same light.
Deep inside, it travels mostly as X-rays. It leaves as sunlight.
The energy is ancient. Its last eight minutes were the trip to you.

SOURCES (for approval — every claim in the script, with the number behind it)

1. "The trip from the Sun to you took eight minutes."
   1 AU = 149,597,870,700 m exactly (IAU 2012 Resolution B2); c = 299,792,458 m/s
   exactly (CODATA 2022, https://physics.nist.gov/cgi-bin/cuu/Value?c). 1 AU / c =
   499.0 s centre to centre; from the photosphere (Sun radius 695,700 km, NASA Sun
   fact sheet, https://nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov/planetary/factsheet/sunfact.html) to
   Earth it is about 497 s, 8 min 17 s. The Earth-Sun distance varies by about
   ±1.7% over the year, so the trip is 8.1-8.4 min; "eight minutes" is the
   rounding. NASA SVS "Origin of Light" (claim 4): "Light takes eight minutes to
   travel" from the Sun to Earth.

2. "The energy ... spent tens of thousands of years, maybe far more, getting out of
   the Sun."
   The hook. The published answers disagree by orders of magnitude, so (A3) the
   script makes the claim every cited source supports: tens of thousands of years
   at the least, "maybe far more" for the higher estimates.

   Lowest cited figure: NASA SVS, "Origin of Light"
   (https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/11084): "Over the course of 40,000 years it will be
   absorbed by other atoms and emitted repeatedly until reaching the sun's
   surface." Tens of thousands.

   Peer-reviewed, read at source on 2026-11-17 (NASA ADS scan,
   https://articles.adsabs.harvard.edu/pdf/1992ApJ...401..759M): Mitalas & Sills,
   "On the Photon Diffusion Time Scale for the Sun", ApJ 401, 759-760 (1992),
   doi:10.1086/172103: "t_d = 1.7 x 10^5 yr", the time "to reach the inner edge of
   the solar convective zone". They call the 3 x 10^3 to 3 x 10^4 yr that the
   common 0.5-1.0 cm step gives "much too short". A fact-checker questioned their
   random-walk factor (eq. 2), which could bring their figure nearer 6e4; either
   way it is tens of thousands or more.

   Agency figure: NASA Marshall Space Flight Center, "The Solar Interior"
   (https://solarscience.msfc.nasa.gov/interior.shtml): "about a million years" to
   reach the interface layer at ~0.7 R.

   Upper end, not re-read at source (Springer refused; bibliographic details
   checked on Crossref): Stix, "On the time scale of energy transport in the Sun",
   Solar Physics 212, 3 (2003), doi:10.1023/A:1022952621810, argues the energy
   transport time is the Kelvin-Helmholtz scale, of order 3e7 years. "Maybe far
   more" covers MSFC and Stix; nothing spoken rests on Stix alone.

   So "tens of thousands of years, maybe far more" is true at SVS's 4e4, Mitalas &
   Sills' 1.7e5, MSFC's ~1e6 and Stix's ~3e7. The hook is about the energy, and a
   viewer who swipes after the first sentence leaves with nothing false.
   On screen: "10,000+ YEARS".

3. "Getting out was the slow part."
   Follows from claims 1 and 2: 8 minutes against more than 1e5 years. No new
   number.

4. "But it is not the same light. Deep inside, it travels mostly as X-rays. It
   leaves as sunlight."
   NASA Marshall Space Flight Center, "The Solar Interior"
   (https://solarscience.msfc.nasa.gov/interior.shtml): the pp chain's second step
   produces "a helium-3 nucleus and a gamma ray"; the energy crosses the radiative
   zone as radiation, "mostly gamma-rays and x-rays", and the outer ~30% by
   convective fluid flows; and the reactions release "the energy that ultimately
   leaves the surface as visible light".
   Deliberately narrower than an earlier draft ("No photon survives the climb",
   "The light is eight minutes old"): no source this pipeline could open states
   that, and NASA SVS "Origin of Light" (https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/11084) describes
   each photon being "absorbed by other atoms and emitted repeatedly until reaching
   the sun's surface". The script now says only what the sources support:
   what leaves is visible light (MSFC), and deep inside (the radiative zone; the
   outer 30% is crossed by convection) the radiation is mostly X-rays. MSFC says
   "mostly gamma-rays and x-rays"; the narrower reading (A3) comes from MSFC's own
   temperatures and Wien's displacement law, b = 2.897771955e-3 m K (CODATA 2022,
   https://physics.nist.gov/cgi-bin/cuu/Value?bwien): peak photon energy hc/(b/T)
   at 15 MK, 7 MK and 2 MK is about 6.4, 3.0 and 0.86 keV, all X-ray energies.
   Gamma rays come from the fusion reactions themselves (MSFC's pp chain).

5. "The energy is ancient. Its last eight minutes were the trip to you."
   Rests on claims 1 and 2. NASA SVS (above) also gives "Light takes eight minutes
   to travel" from the Sun to Earth. No new number.

6. Not spoken: the photon-path animation.
   Zones: NASA MSFC (above) puts the core in the innermost 25% of the radius, the
   radiative zone from 25% to 70%, and the convection zone in the outermost 30%.
   Helioseismology puts the base of the convection zone at 0.713 R
   (Christensen-Dalsgaard, Gough & Thompson, ApJ 378, 413 (1991),
   doi:10.1086/170441). The animation shows a random walk through the radiative
   zone, its colour set by log temperature from the NASA MSFC profile (15 MK at
   the centre, 7 MK at 0.25 R, 2 MK at 0.7 R, 5,700 K at the surface): violet for
   high-energy radiation, yellow for visible light. So the path is only ~26% of
   the way to yellow at 0.7 R, where the radiation is still X-ray, and reaches
   visible light only at the surface. The hue is schematic. Then rising convection cells above 0.7 R, where NASA MSFC
   says "convective motions carry heat quite rapidly to the surface". It is labelled
   on screen as not to scale: a 0.09 cm step across 700,000 km cannot be drawn. The Sun-Earth shot is also labelled not to
   scale (Earth is drawn far too big and far too close).

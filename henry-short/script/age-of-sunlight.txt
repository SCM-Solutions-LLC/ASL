The energy in the sunlight on your skin spent more than a hundred thousand years getting out of the Sun.
The trip from the Sun to you took eight minutes.
Getting out was the slow part.
But it is not the same light.
No photon survives the climb.
The energy is ancient. The light is eight minutes old.

SOURCES (for approval — every claim in the script, with the number behind it)

1. "The trip from the Sun to you took eight minutes."
   1 AU = 149,597,870,700 m exactly (IAU 2012 Resolution B2); c = 299,792,458 m/s
   exactly (CODATA 2022, https://physics.nist.gov/cgi-bin/cuu/Value?c). 1 AU / c =
   499.0 s = 8 min 19 s from the photosphere to Earth. The Earth-Sun distance varies
   by about ±1.7% over the year, so the trip is 8.2-8.5 min; "eight minutes" is the
   rounding. "From the Sun" means its visible surface, which is where the light we
   see leaves.

2. "The energy ... spent more than a hundred thousand years getting out of the Sun."
   The hook, and a deliberate lower bound, because the published answers differ by
   two orders of magnitude depending on what is timed (A3: the script makes the
   narrower claim that every source supports).

   Agency figure, the one this line rests on: NASA Marshall Space Flight Center,
   "The Solar Interior" (https://solarscience.msfc.nasa.gov/interior.shtml): "about
   a million years" to reach the interface layer at ~0.7 R. More than a hundred
   thousand years.

   The two papers below give the published range. Their bibliographic details are
   checked against Crossref; their abstracts could not be reached from this
   pipeline's network on 2026-11-17 (ADS and Springer refused), so the figures
   below are as reported in the literature and not yet re-read at source.

   Photon random walk out of the core: Mitalas & Sills, "On the Photon Diffusion
   Time Scale for the Sun", ApJ 401, 759 (1992), doi:10.1086/172103, bibcode
   1992ApJ...401..759M, get 1.7e5 years. Their contribution is the step length:
   earlier work assumed 0.5-1.0 cm, which makes the diffusion time an order of
   magnitude too short; a solar model gives 0.090 cm, and the shorter step pushes
   the time up to 1.7e5.

   Energy transport: Stix, "On the time scale of energy transport in the Sun",
   Solar Physics 212, 3 (2003), doi:10.1023/A:1022952621810: the time
   scale of energy transport is the Kelvin-Helmholtz time scale, of order 3e7
   years, roughly 100 times the Mitalas & Sills photon-diffusion time, because most
   of the interior's thermal energy is in the particles, not the photons.

   Disagreement (A3): NASA MSFC's ~1e6 years sits between Mitalas & Sills' 1.7e5
   (photon diffusion) and Stix's ~3e7 (energy transport); they time different
   things with different step lengths. The range is 1.7e5 to ~3e7 years. "More
   than a hundred thousand years" is true under every one of them, and the script
   never says which. That is why the hook
   is about the energy, not the light: the light is the part that is eight minutes
   old (claim 4), and a viewer who swipes after the first sentence leaves with
   nothing false.

3. "Getting out was the slow part."
   Follows from claims 1 and 2: 8 minutes against more than 1e5 years. No new
   number.

4. "But it is not the same light. No photon survives the climb."
   NASA Marshall Space Flight Center, "The Solar Interior"
   (https://solarscience.msfc.nasa.gov/interior.shtml): energy crosses the
   radiative zone as radiation, mostly gamma rays and X-rays; the outer ~30% is
   crossed by convective fluid flows; and the energy "ultimately leaves the surface
   as visible light". NASA SVS, "Origin of Light" (https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/11084):
   photons are absorbed and re-emitted repeatedly on the way out. So the light that
   leaves the surface is not the light made in the core, which is the part the
   popular version of this fact drops. The strongest support for "no photon" is
   the hand-off at 0.7 R: above it the energy is carried by moving plasma, not by
   radiation, so no photon from the core can be the one that leaves.

5. "The energy is ancient. The light is eight minutes old."
   Rests on claims 1, 2 and 4. NASA SVS (above) also gives the eight minutes from
   the Sun to Earth. No new number.

6. Not spoken: the photon-path animation.
   Zones: NASA MSFC (above) puts the core in the innermost 25% of the radius, the
   radiative zone from 25% to 70%, and the convection zone in the outermost 30%.
   Helioseismology puts the base of the convection zone at 0.713 R
   (Christensen-Dalsgaard, Gough & Thompson, ApJ 378, 413 (1991),
   doi:10.1086/170441). The animation shows a random walk through the radiative
   zone, its colour set by log temperature from the NASA MSFC profile (15 MK at
   the centre, 7 MK at 0.25 R, 2 MK at 0.7 R, 5,700 K at the surface): violet for
   high-energy radiation, yellow for visible light. So the path is only ~26% of
   the way to yellow at 0.7 R, where the radiation is still X-ray, and reaches
   visible light only at the surface. The hue is schematic. Then rising convection cells above 0.7 R, where NASA MSFC
   says "convective motions carry heat quite rapidly to the surface". It is labelled
   on screen as not to scale and as energy rather than one photon: a 0.09 cm step
   across 700,000 km cannot be drawn. The Sun-Earth shot is also labelled not to
   scale (Earth is drawn far too big and far too close).

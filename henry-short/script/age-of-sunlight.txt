The sunlight on your skin is a hundred thousand years old.
Eight minutes of that was the trip from the Sun.
The rest was getting out.
But it is not the same light.
No photon survives the climb.
The energy is ancient. The light is eight minutes old.

SOURCES (for approval — every claim in the script, with the number behind it)

1. "Eight minutes of that was the trip from the Sun."
   1 AU = 149,597,870.7 km; c = 299,792.458 km/s, so 499.0 s = 8 min 19 s from the
   photosphere to Earth. Uncontested. "From the Sun" means its visible surface,
   which is where the light we actually see leaves.

2. "A hundred thousand years old."
   The softest number in the script, and the one to read before changing a word of
   it: what the answer is depends on which quantity is being timed.

   As a photon random walk out of the core: Mitalas & Sills, "On the Photon
   Diffusion Time Scale for the Sun", ApJ 401, 759 (December 1992),
   doi:10.1086/172103, bibcode 1992ApJ...401..759M, get 1.7e5 years. Their actual
   contribution is the step length. Earlier work assumed 0.5-1.0 cm, which makes
   the diffusion time an order of magnitude too SHORT; a solar model gives 0.090 cm
   instead, and the shorter step is what pushes the time up to 1.7e5. Note the
   direction: the millimetre-scale step is the corrected figure, not the naive one.
   An earlier draft of this file had that backwards.

   As the time for the Sun's energy to get out: Stix (2003, Solar Physics) argues
   the photon diffusion time is not the quantity people mean, because most of the
   interior's thermal energy sits in the random motions of electrons and ions
   rather than in photons. Allowing for that heat capacity puts the thermal
   adjustment time at order 1e7 years, and he quotes 3e7 as the right order of
   magnitude. So the published answers span 1.7e5 to ~1.7e7 depending on the
   question.

   This short is making the photon claim — its whole turn is that no photon
   survives the climb — so Mitalas & Sills is the figure it rests on, and "a
   hundred thousand" is a round number a little under their 1.7e5. What is NOT
   safe is to gloss this as the time for the Sun's energy to escape, full stop:
   that is the reading Stix objects to, and the script's "the rest was getting
   out" sits closer to it than is comfortable. If a citable number is wanted,
   "about a hundred and seventy thousand years" is it, and it would mean
   re-recording the line and re-cutting the ≈100,000 YEARS tag with it.

   Provenance: the Mitalas & Sills entry is confirmed against ADS and Semantic
   Scholar (page range given as 759-760 by Semantic Scholar, 759-761 elsewhere);
   the abstract's own wording is the source for the step lengths and for the
   "too short by an order of magnitude" direction. The Stix figures are from
   secondary reports of that paper, not from the paper itself.

3. "The rest was getting out."
   Core out to ~0.7 solar radii is the radiative zone, where the energy diffuses
   and claim 2's time is spent. From 0.7 R to the surface is the convective zone,
   which carries energy by bulk motion of plasma in weeks to months — negligible
   against the radiative-zone figure. That is why the short animates a long grind
   and then a fast break-out, rather than one uniform crawl.

4. "But it is not the same light. No photon survives the climb."
   Fusion in the core emits gamma rays. Those are absorbed and re-emitted by the
   plasma countless times on the way out, each interaction degrading the energy,
   and what leaves the photosphere is a ~5,772 K blackbody spectrum: mostly visible
   light. One core gamma photon's energy ends up spread over of order a million
   visible photons (~1 MeV divided by ~2 eV). So no individual photon makes the
   trip, which is exactly the part the popular version of this fact drops. It is
   in the script because without it the hook is false.

5. "The energy is ancient. The light is eight minutes old."
   Rests on claims 1, 2 and 4. No new number.

6. Zone fractions used by the animation, not spoken.
   Fusion is essentially all inside 0.25 R; the radiative/convective boundary is at
   ~0.7 R; core ~15.7 million K against a ~5,772 K photosphere. Standard solar
   model figures, uncontested at this precision.

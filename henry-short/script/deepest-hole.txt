The deepest hole humans have ever dug was finished with a bit narrower than a dinner plate.
It took the Soviet Union about twenty years.
It goes down twelve kilometres.
And it never made it through the crust.
At the bottom, the rock was over two hundred degrees.
Drilling stopped in nineteen ninety-two, when its funding was cut.
Twelve kilometres down. The centre of the Earth is over six thousand more.

SOURCES (for approval — every claim in the script, with the number behind it)

Sources read (an independent fact-check, 18 Nov 2026):
- Lobanov, Chicherov & Sharov, Arktika i Sever 44: 267-284 (2021),
  doi:10.37482/issn2221-2698.2021.44.267. Russian Academy of Sciences authors;
  full text. "Drilling was started on May 24, 1970"; "In 1990, the maximum depth
  was reached - 12262 m"; a "214 mm drill bit"; "at a depth of 10 km reached
  180°C, and at a depth of about 12 km ... 212°C, instead of the assumed
  120°C"; "a drastic reduction in funding made it impossible to continue the
  work. Drilling was stopped in 1992".
- Zhu & Huang, Communications Earth & Environment 7: 124 (2026),
  doi:10.1038/s43247-026-03246-z: "reaching 12,262 m between 1970 and 1989";
  "temperatures reached 180 °C at 10 km and 212 °C at 12 km"; "Drilling was
  abandoned in 1992 when unexpectedly high bottom-hole temperatures of 212 °C
  induced plastic rock flow that repeatedly sealed the hole."
- Clarke et al., "The Kola Superdeep Drill Hole by Ye. A. Kozlovskiy (1984): A
  detailed summary", USGS Open-File Report 86-517 (1986), summarising
  Kozlovskiy, Kol'skaya sverkhglubokaya (Moscow: Nedra, 1984): "Crustal
  thickness in the vicinity of the corehole was shown to be about 40 km"; the
  Moho under the Kola Peninsula as a whole is put at 37 km (range 28-40).
- NASA NSSDC Earth Fact Sheet: volumetric mean radius 6,371 km; core radius
  3,485 km.

1. "The deepest hole humans have ever dug..."
   Kola Superdeep Borehole, well SG-3 (the record is its third branch, sidetracked at 7,010 m; Lobanov), Kola Peninsula, USSR (now Russia):
   12,262 m, "which has not been surpassed so far" (Lobanov 2021); Zhu & Huang
   (2026) still treat it as the deepest. The script says "deepest" (vertical
   depth), never "longest".

2. "...was finished with a bit narrower than a dinner plate."
   The hole narrows in steps (Lobanov 2021, Fig. 5, actual well construction):
   720 mm to 40 m, cased at 325 mm to 2,000 m (so bored wider), cased at
   245 mm to 8,770 m, then open hole 215.9 mm to 12,262 m, drilled with a
   214 mm bit (Lobanov). Lobanov also has the bore going oval below 7 km, with
   "many caverns"; the USGS summary lists 295.3 mm reamers and cavities "to
   depths of 7,000 m". So only the last stretch is narrower than
   the 25.6 cm plate (Met 51.86.100); the line says "finished with a bit", and
   the labels name the bit ("21 CM BIT", "THE BIT", "the last stretch").

3. "It took the Soviet Union about twenty years."
   Started 24 May 1970 (Lobanov). The sources disagree on the end: 1989 (Zhu &
   Huang) or 1990 (Lobanov). 19 or 20 years, so the line says "about twenty"
   and the counters end on "1989–90".

4. "It goes down twelve kilometres."
   12,262 m, rounded down. The counter on screen runs to the full figure.

5. "And it never made it through the crust."
   Crust about 40 km thick at the site (Kozlovsky, USGS OFR 86-517), so the hole
   got about 31% of the way through ("about a third" on screen).

6. "At the bottom, the rock was over two hundred degrees."
   About 212 °C at about 12 km (Lobanov; Zhu & Huang), against 120 °C assumed
   (Lobanov). 180 °C was the reading at 10 km, not at the bottom. Celsius; the
   tag says so, the voice does not.

7. "Drilling stopped in nineteen ninety-two, when its funding was cut."
   Lobanov: "a drastic reduction in funding made it impossible to continue the
   work. Drilling was stopped in 1992"; a casing run in 1992 was meant to carry
   drilling on to 13 km. Zhu & Huang put the end on heat, which "induced
   plastic rock flow that repeatedly sealed the hole" (that sentence carries no
   citation of its own); Lobanov does not mention plastic flow. The line states only what Lobanov states; the
   heat is in line 6.

8. "The centre of the Earth is over six thousand more."
   Mean radius 6,371 km (NSSDC); 6,371 - 12.262 = 6,359 km still to go, so "over
   six thousand more" (on screen "6,000+ KM MORE"). The hole is 0.19% of the
   way to the centre, shown as "0.2%".

9. Figures used by the animation, not spoken.
   Depth against year (Lobanov): 7,263 m in May 1975, then almost a year's
   pause to change rigs; 9,584 m in June 1979 (passing the US record of
   9,583 m); 12,000 m on 27 December 1983, 12,066 m before the 1984
   drill-string break, which sent drilling back to 7 km; the third branch
   reached 12,262 m in 1989-90. The illustrated look's depth counter runs on
   the same clock as its year counter through those points, straight between
   them, and from 1984 to 1989 it holds at the deepest point reached so far,
   labelled as such. The core is drawn to
   3,485 km (NSSDC); no inner core is drawn; crust drawn at 40 km. At the size the Earth is drawn the crust is under two
   pixels thick and the hole is a fraction of one, which is why the last shot
   labels the hole instead of drawing it.

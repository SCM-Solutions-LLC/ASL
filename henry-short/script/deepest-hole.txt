The deepest hole humans have ever dug is as wide as a dinner plate.
It took the Soviet Union nineteen years.
It goes down twelve kilometres.
And it never made it through the crust.
At the bottom, the rock was a hundred and eighty degrees. Too hot to keep drilling.
Twelve kilometres down. The centre of the Earth is six thousand more.

SOURCES (for approval — every claim in the script, with the number behind it)

Provenance for the whole file: these figures were written from the standard
published account of the Kola Superdeep Borehole (Kozlovsky, ed., "The Superdeep
Well of the Kola Peninsula", Springer 1987; Kozlovsky, "The World's Deepest
Well", Scientific American 251(6), December 1984) as it is commonly reported.
The depth, the dates and the Earth's radius are uncontested; the soft numbers
are flagged where they appear.

Checked 9 Nov 2026 against secondary sources (the Wikipedia article as returned
by search, HowStuffWorks, Works in Progress, EarthDate), not against Kozlovsky:
confirmed are the 24 May 1970 start, 12,262 m, the 23 cm bore, 180 °C against
an expected 100 °C, the 1992 stop, the 6 June 1979 passing of Bertha Rogers at
9,583 m, 12,000 m in 1983, and that it is still the deepest hole by true
vertical depth. Not confirmed: the 35 km crust figure (claim 5) and the 300 °C
projection (claim 7). No spoken line changed.

1. "The deepest hole humans have ever dug..."
   Kola Superdeep Borehole, branch SG-3, Kola Peninsula, USSR (now Russia):
   12,262 m true vertical depth, reached in 1989. Still the deepest point ever
   reached by drilling. Most sources say 1989; at least one Russian-authored
   account says 1990. The script does not speak the year, but "nineteen years"
   (claim 3) leans on it.

   "Deepest", not "longest", and the difference is real: several oil wells are
   longer measured along the bore — Sakhalin-I extended-reach wells and Qatar's
   Al Shaheen are past 12 km of measured length — but they run mostly sideways
   and get nowhere near 12 km below the surface. If the line is ever reworded
   to "longest", it becomes false.

2. "...is as wide as a dinner plate."
   The bore is 23 cm (9 in) across at depth. A dinner plate is 25-28 cm, so
   the comparison flatters the hole slightly rather than shrinking it.
   SOFT in one respect: 23 cm is the diameter of the deep section. The top of
   the well is wider and cased, as every deep well is. The tag on screen says
   "23 CM WIDE" with no qualifier; "at the bottom" is the strictly accurate
   reading.

3. "It took the Soviet Union nineteen years."
   Drilling began 24 May 1970; the final depth was reached in 1989. 1989 - 1970
   = 19. Drilling did not stop then: attempts to go deeper continued until 1992
   without passing 12,262 m, and the project was formally closed later. So
   nineteen years is the time to reach the depth, not the life of the project.

4. "It goes down twelve kilometres."
   12,262 m, rounded down. The counter on screen runs to the full figure.

5. "And it never made it through the crust."
   The continental crust of the Baltic Shield at the site is about 35 km thick,
   so the hole got roughly a third of the way through (12.262 / 35 = 0.35).
   SOFT: 35 km is a round figure for the local Moho depth; published estimates
   for the area run to about 40 km. The claim in the script — that it did not
   get through — holds anywhere in that range; only the "a third of the crust"
   tag moves, to "under a third". The check found "about one-third of the
   Baltic Shield's crust" in secondary sources but no measured Moho depth for
   the site, so 35 km is still a recalled figure.

6. "At the bottom, the rock was a hundred and eighty degrees."
   About 180 °C at 12 km, against roughly 100 °C predicted when the well was
   planned. Celsius; the tag says so, the voice does not. Wikipedia attaches
   the 180 °C reading to 11,882 m, where the last branch was abandoned in 1992,
   not to the 12,262 m record branch; both are "the bottom" at the rounding the
   script uses.

7. "Too hot to keep drilling."
   The reason given for stopping: at that temperature the rock behaved more
   like a plastic than a solid and the hole would not stay open, and the
   projected temperature at the 15 km target was around 300 °C, beyond what the
   equipment could work in. Funding after 1991 is also part of why it ended, so
   "too hot" is the engineering reason rather than the whole story.

8. "The centre of the Earth is six thousand more."
   Mean radius of the Earth 6,371 km; 6,371 - 12.262 = 6,359 km still to go, so
   "six thousand more" is a round number on the low side. The hole is 0.19% of
   the way to the centre, shown as "0.2%".

9. Figures used by the animation, not spoken.
   Depth against year: 9,583 m in 1979 (passing the Bertha Rogers well in
   Oklahoma, the previous record), 12,000 m in 1983, 12,262 m in 1989. The
   counter follows those, which is why it races and then crawls. The milestones
   are confirmed (see the provenance note); SOFT only in that the curve between
   them is a straight-line interpolation.

   Earth's layers by radius: inner core to 1,221 km, outer core to 3,480 km,
   mantle above that, crust drawn at the 35 km of claim 5. Standard PREM
   figures. At the size the Earth is drawn the crust is under two pixels thick
   and the hole is a fraction of one, which is why the last shot labels the hole
   instead of drawing it.

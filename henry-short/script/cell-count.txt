By cell count, you are less than half human.
About thirty trillion of your cells are yours.
Thirty-eight trillion are bacteria.
But put them all on a scale, and those bacteria weigh about two hundred grams.
And your own cells are not what you picture. Eighty-four percent are red blood cells.
By count, your cells are mostly blood. And they are outnumbered by their passengers.

SOURCES (for approval — every claim in the script, with the number behind it)

Everything here rests on one paper: Sender, Fuchs & Milo, "Revised Estimates for
the Number of Human and Bacteria Cells in the Body", PLoS Biology 14(8): e1002533
(2016), doi:10.1371/journal.pbio.1002533. Its numbers are for a "reference man"
(20-30 years old, 70 kg, 170 cm) and the authors put the uncertainty at roughly
25%, with person-to-person variation on top (they quote ~50%). Every figure in
the script is therefore a round number for one standard body, not a measurement
of the viewer.

Provenance (checked 9 Nov 2026): 3.0e13, 3.8e13 and 0.2 kg are in the paper's
abstract. The 84% red-blood-cell share, the ~75% muscle-and-fat share of cell
mass, the 25% uncertainty, the ~52-53% person-to-person variation and the
defecation remark were confirmed against the BioNumbers entry for the paper
(ID 113005) and Science News / Weizmann coverage of it. The paper's own full
text and Table 3 were not opened: the PLoS and PMC hosts were not reachable
from the session that did the check. No figure in the script changed.

1. "By cell count, you are less than half human."
   3.0e13 human cells against 3.8e13 bacteria: 30 of 68 trillion is 44%. This is
   the soft one. The ratio is 1.3:1, and the paper's own point is that this is
   close enough to 1:1 that a single bowel movement — which sheds a large share
   of the colon's bacteria — can tip it back to humans being the majority. So
   "less than half" is true of the reference estimate and within its error bar
   of not being true. What is NOT soft is the direction of the correction: the
   old "bacteria outnumber you ten to one" figure is the myth this paper
   retired, and the short must never be captioned or titled with it.
   The count is of bacteria only, which is what the paper counts; archaea, fungi
   and viruses are not in the 38 trillion.

2. "About thirty trillion of your cells are yours."
   3.0e13 human cells in the reference man.

3. "Thirty-eight trillion are bacteria."
   3.8e13, nearly all of them in the colon. The animation scatters them through
   the figure because it is a count, not a map; it does not claim they live
   everywhere.

4. "Those bacteria weigh about two hundred grams."
   Total bacterial mass ~0.2 kg, about 0.3% of a 70 kg body. They are that light
   because a bacterium is of order a thousand times smaller by volume than a
   typical human cell — which is why the animation shrinks them as they land on
   the scale. This also replaces an older folk figure of 1-2 kg.

5. "Eighty-four percent are red blood cells."
   ~2.5e13 red blood cells of 3.0e13 human cells, by COUNT. The unit chart shows
   25 of 30 dots, which is 83%; the label carries the paper's 84%.

6. "By count, your cells are mostly blood."
   True by count only, and the line says so. By MASS it is the other way round:
   muscle and fat cells are about three quarters of the body's cell mass, and
   red blood cells only a few percent, because they are among the smallest
   cells there are. An earlier draft said "You are mostly blood", which is false
   without the qualifier and was changed for that reason.

7. "And they are outnumbered by their passengers."
   Claim 1 again, with the same caveat.

8. The unit chart, not spoken.
   68 dots, one per trillion cells: 30 human (25 of them red blood cells) and 38
   bacteria. The picture is exact to the rounding of the numbers above.
